title: "What a State It Was"
category: "story"
subtitle: "Some things hurt more because nobody ever said them out loud."
---

I started noticing some changes.

Not something very big at first, but small changes in the behaviour, the pattern, the way you treated me. The way we talked. The way you replied. The way somehow I started feeling that I was not as important as I used to be.

I thought maybe it was just a phase.

Maybe you were busy.

Maybe something was going on.

Maybe I was just overthinking.

I kept giving reasons to myself because perhaps accepting the obvious thing was much harder.

And then somehow I got to know that there was someone else.

Someone who had somehow overtaken my name in your priority list.

You told me you were busy, but then I found out that you were talking to X.

As if the business had suddenly vaporised.

And honestly, I started hating X in my head.

I never showed it.

Not to you.

Not to him.

I never complained. Never made a face. Never said anything that could make him feel that I had some problem with him.

Because technically, what had he even done?

Nothing.

That was the frustrating part.

He hadn't done anything wrong.

He was just there.

And somehow his existence started bothering me.

It started making me crazy.

You became distant.

Our conversations slowly became hollow.

The same person was there, the same words were being exchanged, but something had disappeared from between them.

I kept thinking that maybe it would change.

Maybe I just needed to give it some time.

Maybe things would come back to how they were.

But the only change that happened was that I kept going down your priority list.

It felt like the market had found a new hero.

And the older one was slowly being given no role.

And then came the day when I found out that you had become closer to him than you had ever been to me.

That completely devastated me.

I don't think I had ever felt that kind of restlessness before.

It was beyond the point where I could simply tell myself to calm down.

That night, for the first time, I couldn't sleep.

Everything was going around in my head.

Every conversation.

Every message.

Every little change I had noticed.

Every time I had told myself that I was probably imagining things.

Nothing seemed more important than drafting messages.

Harsh messages.

Messages that sounded like ultimatums.

Messages where I was saying,

*"I don't want to talk to you anymore."*

And that was probably the biggest lie I could have written.

Because it was the farthest thing from the truth.

I didn't want to stop talking to you.

I wanted the exact opposite.

I wanted you to notice.

I wanted you to ask me what happened.

I wanted to tell you everything, and at the same time I wanted to tell you nothing.

What a confusing state to be in.

I cried.

And for once I didn't try to stop it.

I let my tears also take part in the grief.

There was so much sadness that I genuinely felt I could not take any more.

Even seeing our previous texts would trigger something.

Sometimes I would just see him online and my mind would start going to places it shouldn't.

I would think the worst things.

Things that were probably not even true.

But at that moment the mind doesn't care much about truth.

It just wants an explanation.

And the worst part?

He was my friend.

I cared about him.

And at the same time I was jealous of him.

How can you care about someone and be jealous of them at exactly the same time?

I don't know.

But I did.

And then I came to know something even more strange.

He was actually unaware that you were that close to him.

He wasn't even particularly comfortable with it.

He told me that sometimes he wanted to tell you to stop bothering him, but he didn't because it would be rude.

And I remember thinking...

What a state it was???

I liked you but couldn't say it.

You liked him but couldn't say it.

He liked somebody else.

And somehow both of us knew it.

Three people.

Three different directions.

And nobody saying the one thing that was actually going on.

When we say that we "can't say it", what does that even mean?

Because sometimes we have already said it.

Just not with words.

It comes out in gestures.

In the way we look at someone.

In the things we remember.

In the unnecessary concern.

In the jealousy.

In the way we suddenly become quiet.

In the way our behaviour automatically gives away something that our mouth is trying very hard to hide.

So were we fools for not reading between the lines?

Or was everything actually too obvious from our side, but not from theirs?

Or maybe...

maybe we knew.

Maybe all of us knew more than we wanted to admit.

And maybe we simply chose to ignore it.

Because accepting something doesn't always make it easier.

Sometimes denial is just the heart's way of buying a little more time.

Days passed.

But the thoughts kept coming back from time to time.

Almost as if they were returning to their home.

I would be perfectly fine for a few days, and then suddenly something would remind me of everything again.

A message.

A name.

An old conversation.

A small thing that shouldn't have mattered.

And it would matter.

But slowly, something changed.

Not the situation.

Me.

I started understanding what people mean when they say,

> *Time will heal, however deep the wound is.*

I used to think that was just one of those things people say when they don't know what else to tell you.

But maybe there is some truth in it.

Time doesn't really erase anything.

It just makes the memory less sharp.

Friends came.

New people came.

New conversations happened.

Life started filling the spaces that had been occupied by one person for far too long.

And slowly my mind learned that it could think about something else.

I could laugh without feeling guilty.

I could have a good day without wondering what was happening somewhere else.

And eventually, I could talk to him again.

Genuinely.

Not pretending.

Not trying to hide some resentment behind a smile.

Not secretly comparing myself with him.

Just talk.

Like a friend.

And I think that was when I realised that perhaps I had healed a little.

Not because I stopped caring.

Not because I suddenly stopped liking you.

Not because everything became meaningless.

But because I no longer needed the situation to change for me to be okay.

Maybe that is what healing actually is.

You don't forget.

You don't suddenly become indifferent.

You just become capable of carrying the memory without letting it crush you.

And perhaps one day, when I look back at all of this, I won't remember the sleepless night or the messages I almost sent.

Maybe I'll just remember that there was a time when three people were standing in the middle of a very strange little story...

each carrying something they couldn't say,

each trying to pretend that nothing was happening,

and somehow,

all of us were saying it anyway.
