it isn’t easy to be _silent._ yet, in silence, the secrets of living and dying quietly reveal themselves.

the art we never learn in our schools, the art of entering, discovering, and abiding in silence, especially at the time of death. 
life itself would appear differently.

death is simply the end of biological activity. it can arrive anytime, untimely, unexpected, indifferent to success or failure. even at the height of joy, at the peak of achievement, or in the prime of life, death makes no distinction. even natural death is a sorrow that cannot be rushed, only lived through. the person we once referred to by name becomes only a body. all achievements, honours, and memories fall away. what disappears is the spark we call life. who has gone? where do they go? we rarely ask. we avoid the topic as if death were unnatural, when in truth it is the most natural event we will ever encounter. it happens to all, and in nature’s perspective, it is necessary.

witnessing a body decay, we instinctively cover our noses and turn away. that same fate awaits every one of us. it is a harsh truth only for those who refuse to accept it.

the body burns and dissolves into nothing. a life that began by crawling returns to emptiness. why are we born? surely not merely to die. there must be meaning. we seek it, like the six blind men describing an elephant, each grasping a part, none seeing the whole. life and death are like that, mysterious, resistant to being captured. the moment we try to define life too tightly, it slips away.

to understand anything, we must let go of what we already think we know. letting go is a kind of death, and without it, nothing new can be born. death renews life. it is the only way learning truly happens.

science and evolution offer explanations, yet your very birth remains a mystery. every today carries the death of yesterday. your childhood had to die for adolescence to appear. adolescence died for adulthood. this movement continues endlessly. before birth, you existed as potential in your father. before that......., ultimately in the very beginnings of the universe.

instead of mourning impermanence, we could see ourselves as continually giving way to new creation. the plants we nurture, the people we meet, the trees we admire, everything is in the process of becoming. leaves turn green, yellow, red, brown, then fall. winter strips a tree bare, yet even that barrenness has a stark *beauty.* death is not a noun but a verb, a process. every name we give is only a temporary label on something that is always changing, evolving, regenerating.

if i am the leaf, i have no date of birth or date of death. i am eaten by insects, i decay into soil, i become the foundation for new life. i live and die moment by moment. why would a leaf fear death? it meets death only when it arrives. death does not occupy its mind. so it truly lives, simply for the joy of living. it is wiser than us. it lives fully in each moment, while we live as though life is guaranteed tomorrow. we crave permanence, reject change, resist death, and in doing so, resist life.

when we see people dying, we immediately turn to what the media shows about us. even there, we cling to our names, our identities. many do not die peacefully, many do not even live peacefully. we worry ourselves to death without ever tasting peace.

lets do one small exercise.

make a fist, hold it tight, and then release it. 


that simple act of letting go brings an unexpected freedom. let go of your wants, your name, your family, your concerns, even your breath, and ultimately yourself. what remains is a cleansed hand, capable of holding both life and death. in every ending lies renewal.

life must be rediscovered moment to moment. it cannot be assumed or possessed.

ask a child how life is going. a child won’t answer, they are too busy living. if you say you “know” life, you have already stopped living it. three meals, clothing, shelter, sex, work, entertainment, and repetitive thinking, this is not life. it is merely resistance of existence.

life must be discovered. to discover it, you must set aside everything you have collected, your beliefs, your customs, your memories, your ideas. until you put aside what you carry from the past, you will never meet life as it is. 

the child wants to explore, he wants to walk he doesn't want to learn it by an app. he does not have a manual but he just knows, so why cannot we live like a child. as he grows into an adult he becomes like us. just like 80 year old retired people. what are we losing, it is life.

we do have the ability to become young once again like a child. we can shed the old habits, dysfunction and nurture habits and attitudes that make us  glowing. it's our life, we need to do it by ourselves. we are all going from dust to dust and the difference is just in the details. how you die depends entirely on how you live. <This message was edited>
