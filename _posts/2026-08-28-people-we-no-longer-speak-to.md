title: "People We No Longer Speak To"
category: "thought"
subtitle: "Some people leave quietly. The mind does not."
---

I still think about people I no longer speak to. Not in a way that ruins my day (exceptions are there though) or makes me want to go back. They just appear sometimes, in the middle of something ordinary. Walking to hostel. Looking out of the window. Halfway through a song I had once shared with them.

And for a few seconds, they exist beside me again, exactly as they used to.

Sometimes I wonder where they are now. How much they’ve changed. Whether they still listen to the same music, disappear for hours before replying, or laugh at the same stupid things. Whether life has rearranged them into someone I wouldn’t recognise anymore.

And strangely, I want to know.

Not because I want everything back. Just because some part of me still wonders what happened to the person who once knew so much about mine.

There is some hearsay that when someone suddenly crosses your mind, it means they’re thinking about you too. I don’t know if I believe it. Probably not. But I like the thought of it.

So whenever someone returns to my mind, I pause for a second and quietly hope life is being kind to them wherever they are.

Sometimes it’s the smallest things I remember. The way they laughed. A particular expression. The name I had told them (I am a person of thousand names). Some completely useless detail that has somehow survived everything else.

It’s strange how you can forget a conversation but remember the way someone looked when they were trying not to laugh.

I think that’s how people stay with us.

They leave little pieces behind. A song. A phrase. A place. A stupid joke. And years later, something brings one of those pieces back.

I think part of growing up is realising how many people we lose without ever deciding to lose them.

There is rarely a final conversation. One missed reply becomes a week. A week becomes a month. Life gets busy. You think you’ll talk tomorrow.

And then one day, you realise you haven’t spoken in a year.

Perhaps the strangest part is that you can miss someone terribly and still not reach out.

Sometimes you want to.

You want to type their name. Send them something only they would understand. Ask how they are. Hear their voice again.

But then you wonder what exactly you are missing.

Them?

The friendship?

Or simply the version of yourself that existed around them?

I still reach out sometimes. Conversations after years can be strange. Both of you type a little too carefully, trying to figure out how much of the old familiarity is still allowed.

But every once in a while, someone replies warmly.

And for a few minutes, nothing feels like it changed.

Maybe some connections don’t disappear.

They just hibernate.

I think that’s why I still care about the people I no longer speak to. Not because I want everything back exactly the way it was. Some things ended for a reason.

But there are still people I would give almost anything to sit with again for one completely ordinary evening.

No explanations. No catching up.

Just sitting there, watching them do something completely ordinary and thinking,

*I remember this.*

*I remember you.*

<div class="post-ending">

<p>And maybe that is the cruelest part of remembering.</p>

<p>You can learn to live without someone.</p>

<p>You can stop waiting for their messages.</p>

<p>You can build a life where their absence no longer feels like an absence.</p>

<p>And still, somewhere inside you, they remain.</p>

<p>Not as they are now.</p>

<p>But exactly as you remember them.</p>

<p>And sometimes, that is enough to make you miss them all over again.</p>
