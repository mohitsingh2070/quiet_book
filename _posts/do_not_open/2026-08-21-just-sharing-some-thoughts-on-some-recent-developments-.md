just sharing some thoughts on some recent developments when i was a bit _uncentered_. 

What unsettled me the most was not even the phase itself, but the way I was collapsing under it. I was going through a rough time, unable to overcome the agony from my everyday life. my mind actually became a bit theatrical in such moments. I became unbearably melodramatic and melancholic with my friends and with the people around me. In a fit of exhaustion and despair, I even started saying I was done with people altogether, sending parting messages as though the world itself was ending around me.


i just was having some reflections today, i remembered and revisited the episode when sita was in the forest and she hears the voice of maricha disguised as rama’s faint and wailing cry.
she gets agitated, worried, frightened. on top of that she quarrels and fights with lakshmana to go and help him. and honestly, just the thought itself that someone whom you love deeply might be in danger, might be at the verge of his life somewhere far away in the forest, that thought alone can shake a person completely.

and this is all happening while she herself is already going through a phase of life she perhaps never had even imagined for herself. exile, uncertainty, discomfort, fear… everything served together.

yet what amazes me is this, that in the midst of all that emotional chaos, when the bhikshuk (ravana disguised as a brahmana) comes there, she still had the calmness and clarity of mind to serve the athithi. somehow she still remembered and practiced the grace in the eye of cyclone.


And here I was facing not even a fraction of that suffering, not even 0.1% of such turmoil yet losing myself so completely that I was throwing pieces of my grief at everyone around me, as if pain itself had become my only language.

It is something I genuinely need to sit with and mull over. Because sometimes suffering does not reveal how deep our pain is, it reveals how fragile our inner balance actually was all along.
