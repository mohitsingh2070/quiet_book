the name of the temple was -

Dazaifu Tenmangu.

I didn't know much about the place before going there.

(Actually, I knew almost nothing.)

But there was this tree there.

A plum tree. (Not the one that i shared)

And somehow, there was a whole story behind it.

More than a thousand years ago, there was a man called Sugawara no Michizane.

He was a scholar, a poet, a politician.

And apparently, he was too good at what he did.

Which, as it usually happens with people,
made some other people uncomfortable.

The Fujiwara clan had him falsely accused
and he was sent away from Kyoto,
to Dazaifu.

Exiled.

Before leaving Kyoto,
he went back to his garden.

There was a particular plum tree there
that he loved.

He had spent years sitting beneath it,
writing poetry.

And knowing that he was leaving it behind,
he wrote - (loose translation)

 "If the east wind blows, send your fragrance to me, O plum blossoms. Though you miss your master, do not forget the spring."

And then comes the part I really liked.

The story says the tree missed him so much
that it couldn't stay behind.

So one night,
it uprooted itself

and flew.

From Kyoto all the way to Dazaifu.

(I know. A tree flying across Japan sounds like something someone added later to make the story better.)

But apparently,
it landed here.

Right beside where Michizane was living.

And that is why it came to be known as Tobiume.

The Flying Plum Tree.

Today, there is a descendant of that very tree
standing in front of the main sanctuary.

And apparently,
it is still the first plum tree in the region to bloom every spring.

I don't know how much of the story is true.

Maybe none of it.

Maybe all of it.

But standing there,
looking at a tree that people have been telling this story about for more than a thousand years,

I was thinking about how strange people are.

We leave places.

We leave people.

Sometimes we are forced to leave.

And sometimes,
even when we leave,

something of us keeps trying to find its way back.

Maybe that is what we call memory.

Or longing.

Or love.

I don't know.

I just know that today,
I stood in front of a plum tree

and thought about someone who left Kyoto a thousand years ago,

and somehow,
his story was still here.
