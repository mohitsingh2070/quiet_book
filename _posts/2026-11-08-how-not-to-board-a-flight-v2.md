*How Not to Board a Flight*

Going on a trek on 7th November (Saturday). Everything was planned.

Well, kind of.

We hadn't planned where to stay or what to eat. But we had a plan for that too:

**Plan as we move!**

Me, Chetan and Thejas. The trio of trekkers.

If you have watched *Hera Pheri*, you can probably guess the kind of coordination we were going to have. Not necessarily in order, and definitely not in calibre, but the trio was there: Baburao, Shyam and Raju. I will leave it to you to decide who was who. (I am obviously not Baburao. Or maybe I am.)

Now, I also had to go home. That's how you know I am from Bihar. You know why.

The cheapest flight, as usual, was on Diwali day — Sunday, 8th November. So I asked my father to book the cheapest one, and he did.

And then came the important question:

**To trek or not to trek?**

As usual, I took my chances. I thought I could return early on Sunday morning and somehow catch the flight, which was at 7 AM.

Seems doable, right?

Let me explain the intricacies of this very sensible plan.

I checked the buses from Kalasa. There was one at 2 PM, and the others were at night. My initial thought was that if we reached the trekking point early, we could start by 6 AM. Thejas, however, informed me that this was unlikely because permission, verification and all the other formalities would itself take about an hour.

So, best case, we start at 7:30 AM. If we walk fast, we could be back by 2 PM.

The problem? It was a 16 km trek. And after that, I would have to catch a bus with my very limited Kannada skills.

Thejas and I were both a little sceptical.

So we came up with Plan B. I would take the night bus, which was supposed to reach Majestic at around 4:16 AM. I would request *anna* (the driver) to drop me near Yeshwantpur, go to campus, collect my things and take a cab to the airport. I had some check-in baggage, as I couldn’t travel light.

Simple.

Wait a minute. The flight wasn't to Patna. It was to Kolkata. From Kolkata, I had to get to the railway station and catch the Vande Bharat.

See? Simple.

As the day approached, my concerns started increasing. Would I actually make it? Then I had another thought. If someone could bring my luggage to the gate, I could save some time.

I asked Manasa and Venkatesh. Both were happy to help. And that gave birth to another brilliant idea: I would hand over my check-in luggage to them, Manasa would keep it in her room, and I would collect it in the morning.

Amazing planning. Truly.

Let's see how that went.

## The journey begins. Eventually.

Our bus from Malleswaram was at 8:42 PM on Friday.

We were waiting for Thejas. At some point, Chetan and I thought we could move to 18th Cross and wait there. Then Thejas called and asked where he should park his vehicle — A Block or B Block.

We told him we had already left, so he could do whatever he wanted.

He asked us to wait near the library and said he would park there. Then, after what felt like an eternity, he called again and asked us to proceed to Maramma Gate. He would park there instead.

By then it was 9:25 PM. We were worried.

Every scooter entering the gate started looking like Thejas. We even asked the security guard that he is our friend. (This was mostly to avoid the whole ID-card checking business.)

Luckily, he arrived, and we finally boarded the bus.

Alas, Thejas had forgotten his trekking stick on the scooter.

He ran back, retrieved it and somehow made it in time.

Now the journey could begin.

A little late, obviously. We were in Bengaluru. Traffic is not an obstacle here; it is a permanent member of the travel group.

We crawled. And crawled. And then crawled some more. At some point, we simply lost hope.

Cut to the chase: we reached Kalasa at around 7:30 AM.

We freshened up as much as one can after a night in a bus, and went for breakfast. Thejas enquired about the bus to Balur, the nearest bus stop to our trekking point —> about 9.5 km away.

Luckily, the bus arrived around 7:50 AM, and we boarded it. We also packed lunch from there.

On the bus, the other two booked their return tickets from Horanadu for 9:15 PM.

A small detail about my ticket, which will become important later: the bus I had booked was a different one, a little earlier than theirs. I vaguely remember receiving a seat-transfer message. Something along the lines of:

> KSRTC Seats Transfer Alert: PNR NO: KS24210767  
> FROM trip CODE: 2032HAOBNG  
> OLD Seat NO: 4, Dep TIME: 08:32 PM  
> TO trip CODE: 2201HAOBNG  
> NEW Seat NO: 3, Dep ...

At the time, I did not give it much importance.

I should have.

## The trek, and the first change of plans

We reached the bus stop quite late. From there, we had to take a jeep to the base camp, which was another 8 km or so inside the forest.

The jeep was going to cost us ₹2,000. After some negotiation, we managed to bring it down to ₹1,300.

For details, contact us. We are now experienced in jeep negotiations.

The three of us reached the base camp at around 9:20 AM. There was verification, allocation of guides and the usual formalities. If I remember correctly, we finally started around 9:45 AM.

At this point, there was no way we were getting back to Kalasa by 2 PM.

Lol.

Anyway, we were told that wherever we were, we had to start heading back towards the base camp by 1 PM. Which meant that if we were halfway up, we would have to stop and return from there.

Our goals changed.

Now we just wanted to reach the peak. Otherwise, all this effort would feel like a defeat.

We started moving fast. And believe me, at every point I was concerned about Chetan. I wasn't sure he would be able to make it. The trek was moderately difficult, and we stayed together —> meaning I was ahead, while Chetan and Thejas were usually together.

Our boss had instructed us not to take photographs until we reached the peak.

A few minutes later, we spotted him doing exactly that.

Rules are important, but apparently they are more important for the people who make them.

Chetan became our unofficial photographer, with full freedom to click and vlog. He took the responsibility very seriously.

The distance markers were another source of entertainment.

One marker would say 2.8 km to go. We would walk half a kilometre, feeling that we had made some progress, and the next marker would announce that we still had 3 km left.

I don't know how that mathematics worked.

But we kept going. Drinking breaks, motivational speeches, and the thought of food kept us moving.

Chetan had brought dry fruits —> almost a kilogram of them, if I remember correctly. At this point, food was not just motivation; it was the entire purpose of the expedition.

Chetan and I reached the peak first. Thejas was somewhere behind us.

We waited, took some photographs, and I had already started eating. We thought Thejas might have stopped somewhere, so I messaged him and asked him to have his lunch.

And then, through the fog and clouds, he appeared.

A dramatic entry.

Like in *Kung Fu Panda*: the dust settles, the picture becomes clear, and there he is.

All three of us had made it.

We stayed there for a while, took photographs and enjoyed the view. At around 1:25 or 1:30 PM, we started descending.

So much for the strict 1 PM deadline. It was more like Indian law, strict but hard to enforce.

We reached the base camp at around 4:40 PM. We refilled our bottles from the water filter, which had miraculously started working. Earlier it had refused to give us water. Maybe it also needed some motivation.

We were supposed to wait for the other group with whom we had come. But time was running out, so we decided to hire a jeep ourselves.

That is where the extra ₹300 plus ₹1,000 came in.

The jeep took us back to the bus stop, where we waited for a bus to Kalasa.

There was a puppy there, looking at us with the most sparkly eyes and making the sweetest little squeaking sounds.

Thejas went to buy biscuits for it.

And just then, the bus arrived.

We boarded without giving the puppy anything.

I still think about that puppy. The bus timetable won that round.

## The bus that changed its mind

We reached Kalasa at exactly 6:02 PM.

We ordered two lime juices and one buttermilk. I put my phone on charging and inquired about the bus to Horanadu, because my bus (and theirs) was supposed to start from there.

The person at the shop told me it would arrive at 6:15 PM.

I also insisted that I wanted lime water, not soda. So he had to prepare it from scratch.

For them, the soda lime comes pre-prepared...

While I was having my lime juice, the bus arrived. I ran over and asked the driver — not the conductor — whether it went to Horanadu.

He said yes.

I asked him to wait for two minutes.

I came back, saw Chetan holding my bag, grabbed it and ran towards the bus.

And then I realised my phone was still inside the shop.

And my lime juice.

I ran back and collected one of them. You know which one.

The conductor was understandably annoyed. I had managed to create an unnecessary traffic situation in the middle of all this.

Anyway, we reached Horanadu.

We took a bath and got ready to visit the temple. My bus was scheduled for 8:32 PM, so we were there by around 7 PM. Thejas and I went inside, while our bags were guarded by the photographer bhaiya.

The queue was huge. The temple meal — prasada — was supposed to be served around 7:30 PM, but that day it started early.

We went inside to have the meal and then realised that we could have called Chetan too, along with the bags. Thejas went and asked him to come. Luckily, there was a cloakroom in the temple.

We all had our meal and went outside. Thejas went back in to collect the prasada.

And I, meanwhile, was checking the bus details.

That is when I read the full message.

> **KSRTC Seats Transfer Alert**  
> PNR NO: KS24210767  
> FROM trip CODE: 2032HAOBNG  
> OLD Seat NO: 4, Dep TIME: 08:32 PM  
> TO trip CODE: 2201HAOBNG  
> NEW Seat NO: 3, Dep ON: 07-Nov-2026 AT 22:01  
> From: ksrtc.in

Please read the full message.

My bus was no longer at 8:32 PM.

It was at **10:01 PM**.

Lol.

The timings had shifted. And just like that, the entire carefully constructed plan started collapsing.

## *The domino effect*

I panicked.

There was no point pretending otherwise. The whole chain of plans — the night bus, the early arrival, the luggage handover, the airport cab, the flight — seemed to crumble at once.

What could I do?

Thejas returned, and at that moment I was calling Air India Express customer care to check whether there was any alternative flight from Bengaluru to Kolkata in the afternoon.

No luck.

The only other flight was at 8 PM.

That was not going to help me much.

So I asked Thejas to book tickets for us on his bus, if seats were available.

And he did.

And just like that, the three of us were together again.

Baburao, Shyam and Raju.

The sequel to our trekking adventure had officially begun.

The bus was expected to reach Majestic around 5:20 AM. I started building another plan.

I asked my friends whether they could meet me at Maramma Circle with a scooter. I contacted Debdyuti and Kanchana to check whether they had helmets and whether the scooters had enough fuel.

I also looped in Manasa and Venkatesh to see if they could deliver my luggage.

Everyone agreed to help.

The idea was that they would bring my luggage, I would collect it near Maramma Circle, and Thejas would drop me at the airport.

A new plan. A new set of people. A new opportunity for chaos.

The whole night went into planning.

At around 4 AM, the bus was near Makkikeri — roughly an hour before Majestic. And by then I knew the plan probably wouldn't work.

Or maybe it would.

But was it really worth troubling so many people, making everyone wake up early, arrange scooters, carry luggage and coordinate everything, just so I could possibly save a little time?

I decided not to.

Finally, I got down at Goraguntepalya Metro and informed everyone:

**The plan has been dropped.**

I repeat.

**The plan has been dropped.**

No scooters. No Maramma Circle meeting. No luggage relay operation.

Just me, my bags, and whatever the Bengaluru transport system had left to offer.

## One last diversion

Somehow, I managed to get a cab to the airport.

There was one more complication. The airport road was undergoing construction, and a diversion had been introduced from 6th November. This alternative arrangement was expected to remain for around 18 months.

The route I had in mind through Hebbal was the best option under the circumstances. The diversion meant roughly another 30 minutes, and I really didn't have those 30 minutes to spare.

At this point, I had stopped asking whether things could get any more complicated.

I was afraid the universe might take it as a challenge.

I reached the airport and…

Well.

That is another story.

**Wait for the next sequel....**
