title: "People We No Longer Speak To"
category: "thought"
subtitle: "Some people leave quietly. The mind does not."
---

I still think about people I no longer speak to. Not in a way that ruins my day (exceptions are there though) or makes me want to go back. At least that’s what I tell myself. They just appear sometimes, in the middle of something completely ordinary. Walking to hostel. Looking out of the window. Halfway through a song I had forgotten I had once shared with them. And for a few seconds, they exist beside me again, exactly as they used to.

And sometimes, for those few seconds, I miss them more than I would like to admit.

I wonder where they are now. How much they’ve changed. What their days look like. Who they talk to when something exciting happens. Who they complain to when their day goes badly. Whether they still listen to the same music. Whether they still disappear for hours before replying. Whether they still laugh at the same stupid things. Or whether life has slowly rearranged them into someone I wouldn’t recognise anymore.

And strangely, I want to know.

Not because I want to enter their life again. Just because there is still some part of me that wants to know what happened to the person who once knew so much about mine.

Sometimes I wonder if they ever think about me too. Just briefly. While hearing a song I showed them once. While walking past a place we had once talked about. When something happens that they would have immediately told me about. I wonder if, for a few seconds, their mind takes them back to me the way mine sometimes takes me back to them.

There is some hearsay that when a random person suddenly crosses your mind, it means they’re thinking about you too. I don’t know if I believe that. Probably not. But I like the thought of it. Maybe because it makes the distance feel a little less one-sided.

So whenever someone suddenly returns to my mind, I usually pause for a second and quietly hope life is being kind to them wherever they are.

Sometimes I remember the smallest things about people even years later. A particular expression they used. The way they laughed at something that wasn’t even funny. The name I had told them (I am a person of thousand names). Some completely useless little detail that I have no reason to remember anymore, and yet somehow my mind kept it safe.

It’s strange.

You can forget their number. Forget the last conversation you had. Forget what you were even talking about on some random Tuesday three years ago.

But somehow you remember the way they looked when they were trying not to laugh.

Maybe that is what people mean when they say someone stays with you.

They just leave little pieces of themselves behind. A song. A phrase. A place. A habit. A stupid joke. And years later, one of those things catches you completely unprepared.

I think part of growing up is realising how many people we lose without ever consciously deciding to lose them.

There’s rarely a final conversation. No great goodbye. No moment where both people sit down and say, “this is where we stop being part of each other’s lives.”

Life is much less dramatic than that.

One missed reply becomes a week. A week becomes a month. You get busy. They get busy. You think you’ll talk tomorrow. Tomorrow becomes next week. And then one day you realise you haven’t spoken in a year.

You just reply a little later each time until eventually the conversation stops renewing itself altogether.

And perhaps the strangest part is that you can miss someone terribly and still not reach out.

Not because you don’t want to.

Sometimes you want to so badly.

You want to type their name. You want to send that one stupid thing that you know they would understand. You want to ask how they are and, for a moment, hear about their life again. You want to know whether they still sound the same when they say hello.

But then you stare at the empty message box and wonder what exactly you are allowed to miss.

The person?

The friendship?

The version of yourself that existed around them?

Or simply the feeling of being known by someone who no longer knows you?

I still reach out sometimes. Even knowing it probably won’t feel the same. Texting someone after years is strange. Conversations dry out quickly. Both of us type a little too carefully, as if neither of us knows exactly how much of the old familiarity is still allowed.

But every once in a while, someone replies warmly.

And something inside you gives in.

For a few minutes, the years disappear. I stop choosing my words carefully. The conversation slips back into its old rhythm without either of us forcing it there.

And I realise that maybe some connections don’t really disappear.

They just hibernate.

Waiting somewhere quietly inside us, until a voice, a song, a place or a name wakes them up again.

I think that’s why I still care about the people I no longer speak to.

Not because I want everything back exactly the way it was. I know better than that. Some things ended for a reason. Some people belong to a chapter that cannot simply be reopened without changing the whole story.

But still...

there are people I would give almost anything to sit with again for one completely ordinary evening.

Watching them do something completely ordinary and thinking, somewhere in the middle of it, *I remember this. I remember you.*

Maybe that is the part I find hardest to accept.

A stupid little detail your mind refuses to let die.

And for a few seconds, they are here again.

<div class="post-ending">

<p>if you were given just one evening with them again, would you even tell them how much you missed them?

Or would you be too afraid that saying it out loud would make you realise just how much you still do?</p>

<p>And maybe that is the cruelest part of remembering.</p>

<p>You can learn to live without someone.</p>

<p>You can stop waiting for their messages.</p>

<p>You can build a life in which their absence no longer feels like an absence.</p>

<p>But somewhere inside you, they were there.</p>
