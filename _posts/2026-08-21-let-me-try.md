Let me try.

Sometimes life throws a curveball, and our first reaction is simply, *"What?"* Someone says something we never expected. Someone we thought would stay, leaves. We wonder, *"Why would she do that?"* or *"I thought I meant more to him."* In that moment, the hurt feels enormous.

But when enough time has passed, and we've quietly outgrown the crisis, that same *"What?"* often becomes *"So what?"* So what if he chose someone else? So what if she said that? Not because it was false, but because it no longer affect us.

To me, that's the heart of the poem -> the distance between *"What?"* and *"So what?"*

The poet isn't rich, yet he doesn't seem poor. In the same breath, he admits he has little, but also shows he needs little. He isn't pretending that wealth doesn't matter, he's simply not measuring his life by what he lacks. Instead, he keeps pointing us back to what is already here.

I think that's something we all do. We spend so much time longing for what we don't have that we forget to notice what we do,
